ABSTRACT Archived satellite audio provides an opportunity to recover telemetry without repeating a radio
observation. We describe Telemetry Yield, a Rust implementation of a resumable multi-hypothesis receiver
for post-frequency-demodulation recordings. Its progressive portfolio combines complementary frontends,
timing hypotheses, guarded channel estimation from separately received packets, and sequence detection. An
additional experimental receiver evaluates residual prediction and codec-derived reliability proxies. A frozen
interim comparison uses 189 completed observations from a planned cohort of 266 historical CANVAS
recordings, with identical numerical audio supplied to Dire Wolf, gr-satellites, and the native receivers.
The progressive receiver recovers 4541 distinct AX.25 unnumbered-information frames counted within
observations, compared with 2998 in the union of the two external receivers. It adds 1547 frames while missing
four reference frames, giving a net increase of 51.5 percent. The experimental codec-enabled and codecdisabled receivers produce identical packet sets in every completed observation, providing no incremental
packet-yield evidence for codec conditioning in this snapshot. These results support further evaluation of
complementary offline decoding, not universal receiver superiority. The study remains incomplete, reuses a
historically exposed cohort after a performance-only restart, and does not establish spacecraft authenticity,
broad modulation coverage, or deployment readiness.

II. RELATED METHODS AND BENCHMARK CONTEXT

MLSE for channels with intersymbol interference is established theory [1]. Timing recovery likewise has longstanding
methods, including Gardner’s timing-error detector [2]. Estimation in autoregressive noise is also well studied [3]. The
present implementation uses these families as building blocks;
an exact trellis search under an approximate fitted channel is
not an assertion that the physical satellite receiver is globally
optimal.
Dire Wolf’s WA8LMF results document is valuable methodological context [4]. Its January 2019 revision compiles
measurements made by different operators and explicitly
warns about track selection, test conditions, software age,
and the interpretation of small differences. The WA8LMF
source describes a standard 1200-baud packet-radio test and
distinguishes discriminator audio from de-emphasized audio
[5]. These distinctions matter because frontend processing
changes the signal presented to a decoder.
We do not compare our CANVAS packet totals numerically
with that compilation. The present study uses 9600-baud satellite audio, a different corpus, and per-observation exact-packet
deduplication. No Telemetry Yield result on the WA8LMF
test medium is reported here. A future controlled WA8LMF
experiment must pin the medium version and track hashes,
implement and qualify the appropriate 1200-baud audio path,
and replay the identical samples through explicitly configured
receivers. Version-specific raw packet totals from unrelated
tests are not a common performance scale.
The gr-satellites command-line documentation distinguishes complex-input operation from audio input after frequency demodulation [6]. Our replay is in the latter domain.
Consequently, this study does not measure coherent acquisition, raw in-phase/quadrature (IQ) performance, RF sensitivity
in dBm, or a change in required signal-to-noise ratio.
III. PROGRESSIVE RECEIVER ARCHITECTURE
A. INPUT CONTRACT AND BASELINE PORTFOLIO

The evaluated study configuration accepts mono post-FM
audio representing 9600-baud GMSK/G3RUH AX.25 traffic.
Recordings are processed in 6-s windows with a 3-s hop.
Overlap can yield repeated packet detections, which are
removed when forming the observation-level packet set.
Two complementary frontends are retained: a legacy finiteimpulse-response/DC-removal path and a square-pulse path
with four-boxcar DC rejection and causal root-mean-square
normalization. Each frontend evaluates 160 fixed timing
hypotheses: 32 phases at each of five clock errors, −500,
−100, 0, 100, and 500 parts per million. Sixteen Gardner

B. GUARDED CROSS-PACKET CHANNEL ESTIMATION

Native baseline detections are accepted only after a received frame-check sequence (FCS) check and strict AX.25
unnumbered-information (UI) structure checks. Plain nonreturn-to-zero inverted (NRZI) and NRZI/G3RUH hypotheses
preserve the physical symbol coordinates needed for channel
training. An accepted packet supplies a candidate training
span; archive payloads and external-decoder packet bytes are
never supplied to the receiver as training targets.

The implementation estimates the centered three-tap response
and bias using ridge-regularized fitting, at least 128 distinct

an effective post-FM audio model, not a full physical model

Source and target windows must use the same frontend,
be within 60 s, and remain disjoint with at least a 1-s guard.
The nearest eligible source is selected deterministically. Supplemental timing tests combine eight transferred-clock phase
hypotheses and eight locally preferred hypotheses, removing
exact duplicates. A source packet can therefore help model an
adjacent difficult interval without fitting that target interval to
a known desired payload. Temporal separation reduces direct
training leakage but does not make neighboring windows
statistically independent.

A disjoint waveform-validation gate rejects poor models. A

is local model pooling, not a general time-varying channel

The baseline, nearest-anchor, blind, and multi-anchor packet

uses an immutable quick-prefix anchor generation; final supplemental work uses the completed baseline anchor generation.
Newly recovered supplemental packets do not recursively
become new training anchors. No CRC-guided bit repair is performed. Retaining the native baseline prevents a supplemental
branch from deleting its packets, but does not guarantee that
P contains every external receiver’s packet.
D. ANYTIME EXECUTION AND RESOURCE USE

The quick, deep, and full modes change the execution budget
of one finite task bank. Default quick and deep budgets are
3000 and 60000 ms. Preparation, hashing, and checkpoint
verification consume this budget, but scheduling and cleanup
overhead can exceed the nominal deadline; it is not a hard realtime guarantee. A later invocation can resume deeper work
from an existing compatible checkpoint without repeating
committed tasks.
Manifests bind the source, numerical input, executable,
and policy. Immutable tasks, atomic checkpoints, and process
locks protect restart consistency. A cached frontend/timing
representation and reusable trellis buffers reduce redundant
work while preserving floating-point arithmetic and the search
bank. Independent work can execute in parallel. Cache size
and thread count are resource settings, not an authorization
to silently prune hypotheses. Incomplete work is explicitly
different from a completed decode with zero packets.

V. HISTORICAL EVALUATION PROTOCOL
A. COHORT AND EVALUATION EXPOSURE

The planned cohort comprises 266 historical CANVAS observations from 15 UTC dates and 161 ground-station identifiers.
It was amended from 500 selected observations by excluding
234 under the recorded prior-exposure/proximity rule with a
120-min same-satellite guard. It is a selected single-mission
cohort, not a representative sample of all SatNOGS modulation
families or all observation outcomes.
The optimized executable campaign is a disclosed repeat
evaluation after a performance-only restart. Earlier partial
results had been inspected. The executable/build and reportreader changes did not intentionally alter receiver thresholds,
cohort selection, or hypothesis banks, but re-evaluation does
not restore unseen-holdout status. The archived protocol and
exposure record are part of the evidence bundle.
The snapshot cutoff is September 12, 2026, 22:52:19 UTC.
Exactly 189 observations have completed all five arms; 77
planned observations have no complete five-arm result in this
snapshot. They are not assigned zero packets. The interim
complete-case subset is not randomized by completion, so
runtime-dependent selection can bias early estimates. No final
confidence interval or significance declaration is inferred from
it.
B. COMMON INPUTS AND COMPARATORS

FFmpeg converts each original recording once into a common
signed 16-bit PCM WAV, retaining its sample rate and channels.
Each decoder receives identical numerical WAV content.
This explicit benchmark conversion differs from the native
progressive CLI’s usual floating-point OGG conversion. I2
additionally receives codec side information from the original
OGG; this extra information is disclosed, not silently treated
as identical input information.
Dire Wolf runs atest -B 9600 -F 0 -h, with bit
repair disabled. The gr-satellites profile explicitly specifies
FSK at 9600 baud and AX.25 G3RUH framing; it is a
frozen generic link profile, not a claim of complete missionspecific application decoding. Network telemetry submission
is disabled. Binary identities, profile content, arguments, and

The user-requested subgroup is frozen metadata with
waterfall_status=with-signal, not a condition
based on our packet recovery. The full cohort has 24 such
labels, three without-signal labels, and 239 unknown labels.
Sixteen of the 24 signal-labelled observations are complete at

adds 1547 packets relative to B and misses four, yielding a net
1543 packets or 51.47%. There is at least one addition in 107
of 189 observations (56.6%). This is neither a packet success
probability nor a fraction of all transmitted packets recovered:
the transmitted ground truth is unavailable.

In the signal-labelled complete subgroup, P recovers 136
packets versus 87 in B: 49 additions, zero losses, and a 56.32%
net increase. Five of the 16 observations show at least one
addition. These small, exploratory subgroup counts should
accompany the overall metric rather than be promoted as a
stronger independent trial.
I1 and I2 produce exactly identical observation-level packet
sets in all 189 cases, totaling 4703. Therefore the current data
show zero additional union packets from enabling I2 relative
to I1. Relative to P, I2 adds 214 packets while missing 52, a
net 162. P and I2 are complementary and neither observed set
is a superset of the other. These comparisons do not isolate the
causal value of individual components because the portfolios
differ. Selecting their union after observing these data would
be exploratory and would require a newly frozen evaluation
policy.
The progressive arm averages 179.3 s per recording, compared with 3.5 s for Dire Wolf and 3.8 s for gr-satellites
on this shared-host snapshot. Its aggregate wall-time ratio
is approximately 51.0 times Dire Wolf and 47.8 times grsatellites. Although P is faster than the recorded audio duration
in aggregate, that fact does not establish streaming latency,
deadline compliance, or isolated hardware efficiency. The
extra recovery has a substantial computational cost.
A separate performance qualification on two previously
used development recordings, each tested twice in paired order,
measured total progressive wall time decreasing from 2199.86
to 1186.93 s (46.05%). Deterministic result projections agreed
in all four pairs. This supports the specific optimization/build
combination on those controls, not a mathematical zero-loss
guarantee for every possible input or a 46% universal speedup.
This small development comparison is distinct from the larger
field-yield study.
VII. LIMITATIONS AND SUBMISSION REQUIREMENTS
A. VALIDITY AND GENERALIZATION

The present evidence is incomplete, single-mission, and retrospectively exposed. Stations, passes, dates, and repeated
spacecraft content create dependence. A final analysis must
use the registered dependence-aware procedure, include missingness and completion by subgroup, and state its resampling
unit. Treating every packet as an independent Bernoulli trial
would produce misleading uncertainty estimates. A finished
cohort alone does not turn these results into an independent
4

Telemetry Yield

validation.
The paper describes current implementation, not a causal
proof that guarded transfer, blind fitting, or model pooling
explains the observed gain. Matched ablations must retain
common frontends, timing banks, input precision, resource
policies, and acceptance checks while disabling one component at a time. Codec experiments require matched white,
residual-only, codec, and pooled-codec controls with successful and rejected calibrations reported. The observed null I2/I1
packet result must be retained even if later experiments change
it.
Dedicated no-transmission, out-of-band, wrong-profile, and
controlled corrupt-packet tests are needed to bound false
acceptance under the complete search policy. Representative
added and lost packets should be independently traced back to
waveform spans. Semantic consistency checks can strengthen
evidence but cannot substitute for source authentication or
become an undisclosed acceptance filter tuned on the test set.
B. IMPLEMENTATION VERSUS QUALIFIED COVERAGE

The generic Rust receiver contains separate complex-IQ BPSK,
QPSK, and offset-QPSK paths; CSP version 1/2, selected
CCSDS transfer formats, Reed–Solomon, and explicit sparsematrix low-density parity-check facilities are also implemented. These paths have configuration and qualification
boundaries and are not the receiver assessed in Table 1.
This manuscript establishes no orbital QPSK/offset-QPSK
gain, no universal CCSDS coding support, and no automatic
unknown-modulation discovery. Post-FM mono OGG cannot
be reinterpreted as phase-preserving IQ for those coherent
paths.
Deployment qualification additionally requires documented
supported inputs and profiles, reproducible installation, dependency/environment pinning, resource admission and restart
tests, and independent review of the final results. The research
web page accompanying this draft must distinguish available
experimental code from production support or a hosted decoding service.
VIII. CONCLUSION

A resumable multi-hypothesis audio receiver can recover
packets missed by a particular pair of established replay
configurations. In a frozen interim set of 189 CANVAS
observations, the progressive implementation yields 51.5%
more per-observation unique UI packets than their union, at
substantially greater computation cost and with four reference
packets missed. This motivates a complementary offline
recovery workflow rather than a universal replacement claim.
The codec-enabled experimental arm adds no packets over
its codec-disabled control in this snapshot. Completing and
independently auditing the cohort, measuring component
ablations and false acceptance, and evaluating genuinely
separated mission/link data are necessary before stronger
scientific or deployment claims.
