## Abstract

Archived satellite observations provide an opportunity to revisit reception with alternative processing, but fair evaluation requires distinguishing complex samples, processed audio, and previously decoded packets. Telemetry Yield is a developing Rust-based workflow associating receiver attempts and resumable search tasks with input identities and protocol-specific evidence. A completed full-search comparison on 20 public CANVAS recordings produced 139, 86, and 44 strict AX.25 UI observation–packet pairs for the progressive receiver, Dire Wolf, and gr-satellites. These reused development data are not independent confirmation. An alternative innovation receiver produced 138 pairs, with no incremental benefit from experimental codec pooling. Minimal IQ and pre-clock controls reproduce one independently archived packet. On separately reconstructed lossless audio from that IQ, progressive processing recovered two packets, including one additional 264-byte PDU with independently verified received FCS. A matched selected-case ablation recovered the additional packet with sequence estimation but not memoryless slicing. Controlled Vorbis round trips eliminated recovery under the tested receiver configurations. Four complete 600-second synthetic negative controls emitted no accepted frame. Resumed and uninterrupted processing also produced identical three-packet results and equivalent outputs for all 1,596 tasks. Conversely, the limited native paths in a completed private archival campaign yielded no verified UI packets. These findings motivate further evaluation, not universal superiority, algorithmic novelty, or submission readiness.

**Keywords:** satellite telemetry; archival reprocessing; software-defined radio; reproducibility; checkpointing; AX.25.

## 1. Motivation and significance

Satellite observations are costly to repeat: a particular pass, interference environment, and spacecraft state cannot simply be recreated. Reprocessing retained recordings may therefore be useful even when no new receiver hardware is available. SatNOGS distinguishes observation artifacts including IQ, audio, and decoded frames. Its documentation describes archived OGG audio as lossy and potentially usable for further decoding, while retained IQ may be held separately by individual station operators. These representations are related, but not interchangeable. [SatNOGS artifact documentation](https://wiki.satnogs.org/Artifacts).

An archival recovery claim must answer more than how many hexadecimal strings a program emits. It must establish which input was processed, which receiver configuration completed, whether output satisfies the relevant framing and integrity checks, and whether a recovered packet was absent from the comparison output or the original archive. Those last two questions are different. A packet missing from two offline comparator runs may already exist in a station database.

Established receivers are useful components and references, not a single monolithic competitor. Dire Wolf is an existing sound-card packet modem/TNC; gr-satellites provides satellite demodulation and decoding with explicit real-sample and IQ input conventions. In particular, a real audio recording must not be silently supplied as complex IQ. [Dire Wolf project](https://github.com/wb2osz/direwolf), [gr-satellites input documentation](https://gr-satellites.readthedocs.io/en/latest/command_line.html).

The intended software contribution is a reusable, auditable retrospective workflow: preserve inputs, run identifiable processing alternatives, retain frame evidence, resume compatible work, and account for failures without interpreting them as empty successful decodes. This draft does not establish that these engineering ideas are unprecedented. Their research usefulness and distinction from existing archival tools still need a fuller related-work assessment and independent user validation.

## 2. Software description

### 2.1. Implementation and input contracts

The native receiver and the qualification utilities described here are implemented in Rust. They currently exist as versioned research programs and frozen experiment builds, not one verified public release. FFmpeg prepares some audio inputs; established external receivers and the source-compatible SatNOGS reference retain their own upstream implementations and GNU Radio dependencies. Thus, “Rust-based” does not mean the complete experimental environment contains only Rust.

The archival workflow preserves acquired objects and metadata separately from derived samples and results. Input identities record an exact path, byte length, and SHA-256 digest. A result must identify its source representation and transformation: real PCM audio, processed discriminator/pre-clock samples, and interleaved complex IQ are different contracts. Sample rate, format, channel meaning, and any gain or resampling must be explicit. A file extension alone is insufficient.

Receiver configuration and coverage must also be distinguished. Adding a satellite profile supplies parameters to an existing implementation; it does not implement a missing modulation or protocol. A missing gr-satellites definition is a limitation of that additional route, not evidence that a station's independent native SatNOGS route never ran. Historical dispatcher behavior and partially completed campaigns must be audited before using their coverage denominators as reception statistics.

### 2.2. Processing and resumable execution

The working pipeline separates acquisition, sample preparation, receiver attempts, per-frame validation, and comparative aggregation. Native searches can evaluate multiple timing and signal-processing hypotheses and deduplicate accepted results. The progressive audio implementation divides its search into named tasks with input, executable, policy, stage, and window identities. Completed compatible task records support later continuation; final results must equal the union of validated completed-task outputs.

The resume contract matters operationally because a station may make an initial bounded attempt and later allocate more computation to the same recording. It does not make a short budget equivalent to a completed deep search, nor guarantee a particular latency on arbitrary hardware. Partial, timed-out, unsupported, and failed attempts must remain distinguishable from complete attempts that recover no packet. Section 3.4 presents one regression test of semantic resume equivalence, not a universal proof of fault tolerance or real-time scheduling.

### 2.3. Frame evidence and the control-suite gate

The current gate independently reads the actual frozen native result formats rather than accepting a result's summary count or success flag. It checks hash-bound artifacts and source identities, completion, received frame-check-sequence bytes, strict AX.25 UI structure, and exact expected packet sets. Missing artifacts, changed bytes, malformed packets, incorrect FCS, wrong inputs, incomplete processing, missing expected packets, unexpected packets, and absent receiver-specific positive/negative coverage prevent qualification. An empty suite cannot pass vacuously.

The implemented parsers cover basic native, progressive, innovation-union, generic-IQ, and M&M-probe result representations. Parser support is not field qualification of all five receiver paths: the real minimal suite reported below covers only two explicitly named receiver/component configurations. The M&M contract additionally requires the recorded fixed configuration and all 24 branches to complete. Its declared clock is signal-driven, not packet-guided; this declaration and result checking are not cryptographic proof of execution provenance.

Expected truth remains an explicit trust boundary: a manifest author must supply independently justified packet expectations. The gate verifies artifact consistency and packet evidence; it cannot authenticate a physical transmission or establish scientific generalization by itself. Accordingly, its output separates `control_suite_passed` from `publication_ready` and `deployment_ready`, which remain false in the present audit. The rebuilt gate passed 34 regular adversarial unit tests and two explicitly run compatibility checks, recorded in the build/test receipt in Appendix A.

### 2.4. Established receiver methods versus proposed contribution

The added Mueller and Müller (M&M) timing-recovery probe is an implementation and integration experiment. M&M is an established technique already available in GNU Radio; it is not a new algorithm introduced by this work. [GNU Radio M&M clock-recovery API](https://www.gnuradio.org/doc/doxygen/classgr_1_1digital_1_1clock__recovery__mm__ff.html).

The frozen probe evaluates linear/cubic interpolation, four initial phases, and three input gains, giving 24 branches, with fixed loop parameters and plain/G3RUH deframing checks. A parameter bank and a Rust implementation alone do not establish a new scientific receiver principle. The presently defensible contribution candidate is the integration of reproducible search, stage-specific controls, and evidence-preserving replay. A claim of algorithmic novelty would require a separately specified mechanism, prior-art comparison, ablation, and held-out improvement.

### 2.5. Bounded sequence model and separation of evidence

The native symbol-rate sequence component uses the approximation

\[
y_n=b+h_{-1}x_{n-1}+h_0x_n+h_{+1}x_{n+1}+e_n,\qquad x_n\in\{-1,+1\}.
\]

It estimates the three taps and bias from guarded interiors of previously validated symbol spans, rejects rank-deficient or poor fits, and transfers only aggregate model parameters to a separate target window. Twenty symbols are excluded at each training-span boundary, and at least 128 distinct interior symbols are required. Target sequence detection minimizes the finite-memory squared-error objective through a trellis; it does not consult the target CRC while selecting its path. Framing and received-FCS checks occur afterwards. This is a bounded approximate model of the prepared symbol stream, not a coherent-IQ channel model or a proof of optimal detection in real, colored noise.

Sequence estimation in channels with intersymbol interference is established prior art, including the classical maximum-likelihood sequence-estimation formulation. The selected additional-packet ablation tests the usefulness of this mechanism in the present workflow, not invention of sequence estimation. [Forney, *Maximum-likelihood sequence estimation of digital sequences in the presence of intersymbol interference*](https://www2.ensc.sfu.ca/people/faculty/cavers/ENSC805/readings/18it03-forney.pdf).

Separate innovation branches fit bounded autoregressive residual models of order zero to two on chronological training/validation portions of other source spans, whitening the observation and channel together. Optional Vorbis-derived variance weights are a further experimental treatment. Their residual validation is conditional on upstream normalization and source timing already estimated from the source window; it is not an untouched raw-waveform holdout. These branches and blind model fitting must be ablated separately. A good waveform fit, a Viterbi path, or a CRC-accepted source anchor alone does not validate a new target packet or establish a real transmission.

### 3.1. Historical public OGG comparison: 20 CANVAS observations

A preserved campaign selected 20 completed CANVAS observations with available public OGG audio from 2026-09-10, using a time/identifier ordering before audio processing rather than recovered yield or visual signal quality. The selection and original method are documented in the [campaign report](/home/ubuntu/telemetry-yield/reports/satnogs-today20-threeway-20260910.md). Although initially selected before that run's outcomes, these recordings have subsequently informed development. They are now **development/retrospective evidence, not a fresh confirmatory test set**, and concern one mission and one GMSK 9600/AX.25 UI use case.

The experimental receiver, Dire Wolf 1.7 (`atest -B9600 -F0 -h`, with bit repair disabled), and gr-satellites 5.9.0 received the same prepared mono 48 kHz PCM16 WAV for each observation. Input hashes were checked around processing. This equalizes those offline arms' sample representation; it does not make PCM16 conversion mathematically lossless relative to float samples, or reconstruct the station's original IQ path. The gr-satellites arm used its FSK/AX.25 G3RUH component on real audio; it was not the full native SatNOGS production chain.

The primary unit was a unique **(observation ID, FCS-stripped strict AX.25 UI PDU)** pair. A repeated packet in two observations therefore contributes two pairs. |---|---:|---:|---:|

| Observations with at least one accepted PDU | 6 | 7 | 4 |

There were 42 observation–PDU pairs emitted by the experimental receiver but absent from both comparator outputs, and one comparator-only pair absent from the experimental receiver. Recorded wall times include process startup but exclude acquisition and common preparation. ### 3.3. Paired representation diagnostic: lossless audio and Vorbis round trips

To investigate an archival-input failure, the reference audit reconstructed a historical SatNOGS audio branch without Vorbis encoding from the same #5122 IQ. The derived 48 kHz float audio was scaled by `1/(0.9*pi)` before both the lossless test and controlled FFmpeg/libvorbis quality-3 and quality-4 round trips. The decoded test inputs had matching sample geometry. This paired design holds the common pre-encoding gain constant; comparing an unscaled signal only with encoded, scaled signals would confound gain and codec effects.

The same versioned M&M executable and declared 24-branch configuration completed on all three inputs:

| Derived 48 kHz input | Unique accepted PDUs |
|---|---:|
| Scaled lossless float audio | 1, matching the known station packet |
| Quality-3 Vorbis round trip, decoded to float | 0 |
| Quality-4 Vorbis round trip, decoded to float | 0 |

This is a **one-packet diagnostic**, not a multi-observation codec benchmark. The derived files are not the original archived OGG and do not certify an exact reproduction of the production encoder. The result shows that, under this fixed tested receiver, these two controlled lossy round trips changed recovery relative to the common scaled lossless input. It does not prove that no other algorithm could recover the packet from lossy audio, that every original OGG failure has this cause, or that the loss is information-theoretically irreversible. These 48 kHz runs are also outside the narrow 19,200 Hz M&M control contract in Section 3.2.

### 3.4. Resume equivalence on public observation #14967362

An independent regression audit compared a preserved uninterrupted progressive run against a newly completed resumed run on the same prepared PCM16 file, executable, and full session policy. The resumed execution's earlier 900-second budget receipt recorded 787 of 1,596 tasks and two packets; its continuation completed all 1,596 tasks and produced three packets.

Both final result files have identical SHA-256 digests and contain the same three full received-FCS-bearing frames. The reviewer independently checked FCS, input identity, each task's content binding, stage/window/session identity, and the equality of the final union to the union of task outputs. All 1,596 task records per side were semantically equal after recursively excluding only elapsed-time fields. This is stronger than comparing aggregate counts, which could conceal substituted or lost packets.

The verified input was a common prepared PCM16 WAV, not original OGG bytes. The test concerns one source and a fixed binary/policy; elapsed times are deliberately outside the equivalence claim because host loads differ. It provides no new telemetry and is not evidence that a three-second run has the sensitivity of a complete run. It supports the specific engineering property that this interrupted analysis can continue to the same audited task/frame result.

The repaired candidate subsequently completed the same input with the same three packets and all 1,596 task outputs semantically equal to the historical uninterrupted run. That second audit necessarily excludes the intentionally changed executable/session identities as well as timing. It is a regression check, not a claim that the two binaries are identical. ### 3.5. Additional packet and matched sequence-estimation ablation

The full progressive receiver completed all 994 tasks on the scaled lossless 48 kHz reconstruction described in Section 3.3. It recovered two distinct 264-byte AX.25 UI PDUs. [Independent two-packet audit](/home/ubuntu/telemetry-yield/work/canvas-reference-audit-20260911/progressive-two-frame-audit.json).

## 4. Impact, limitations, and remaining evaluation

The concrete engineering findings are exact known-packet controls, a selected additional IQ-derived packet with a matched mechanism ablation, detection of a representation-sensitive failure, and strong single-input resumability regressions. The historical audio cohort suggests that complementary receiver hypotheses can add packets relative to particular offline baseline settings. It simultaneously documents a missed baseline-only packet and much larger recorded computation; the completed private campaign provides a substantial negative result for its limited native paths. These outcomes do not establish universal superiority over SatNOGS, Dire Wolf, or gr-satellites.

The present evidence is concentrated on CANVAS 9600-baud AX.25-family reception. It does not qualify all popular modulation methods, every supported software configuration, standalone CCSDS framing/FEC, or all telemetry types. Unknown or unimplemented framing must remain explicitly unsupported rather than being forced through the AX.25 UI gate. A successful live-IQ decode cannot impose an expected-positive label on archived OGG unless recoverability of that exact representation is independently demonstrated.

Before final evaluation, a single reviewed release and its supported input/protocol matrix should be frozen. Positive and negative controls should cover each claimed path, with independent captures and representative impairments. Final data selection must be prospective relative to tuning, account for unavailable artifacts, and avoid choosing observations by output. ## 5. Conclusions

This development study motivates an evidence-preserving tool for retrospective satellite telemetry analysis. It reports a historical higher-yield but higher-cost comparison, known-packet controls, one additional 264-byte IQ-derived packet, a controlled representation diagnostic, and verified resumable execution. The extra packet does not come from the original OGG; the completed private campaign yielded no verified UI packets through its limited native paths. Independent generalization evidence and a final reviewed release remain incomplete. A defensible future paper could report validated software and measured archival benefit; the current evidence does not support describing the demodulator as revolutionary, universally superior, submission-ready, or deployment-ready.
